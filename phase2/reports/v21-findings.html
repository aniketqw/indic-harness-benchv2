What the corrected benchmark shows

Results from the v2.1 campaign only: 16 synthetic office-automation tasks from eight Indian domains, given to two open models as agents. The instruction (English, Hindi or Hinglish) and the workspace files (English or Hindi) vary independently. Every condition was graded by an exact-match oracle.

  • The language of the files matters more than the language of the request. Hindi workspace files cost about 16 percentage points. Hindi or Hinglish instructions cost about 8 points compared with English.
  • GLM loses on details; Nemotron loses on budget. In Hindi workspaces, GLM's extra failures are mostly partially wrong files. Nemotron's are mostly runs that used the whole output budget without writing anything.
  • The suite is not saturated. Task results range from 5/12 (appointment rebooking) to 11/12, and only 4 of 16 tasks reach 11/12.

Design in brief

  • 16 tasks, two per domain: finance, schools, agriculture, localization, municipal, logistics, clinic, archives. Each task is generated from a fixed seed into one canonical dataset.
  • The English and Hindi workspaces render the same facts. The Hindi one adds Hindi headers and labels, Devanagari digits, Hindi month names and time words.
  • Difficulty comes from rule interaction: unsorted amendment feeds, precedence, inclusive boundaries, unit conversion, rounding, capacity allocation and Unicode normalisation. All rules are stated in the notice, which ends with a glossary of value spellings. Every rule is necessary: ignoring any single one scores below 100.
  • The agent works in a locked-down container with these budgets: 32,768 tokens per response, 65,536 output and 600,000 input tokens, 40 requests and 1,200 s per attempt.
  • Primary outcome: correct output, meaning the result file is fully correct when the attempt ends. Secondary: correct and within budget (142/192).

  • GLM nearly always writes a file. In Hindi workspaces it makes more field errors, with a mean score of 95.7 vs 98.6 in English.
  • Nemotron works through much of the task in its reasoning before writing a script. On Hindi files it produces about 23% more output and takes about 30% longer, and 10 of 48 runs end without a file.
  • Nemotron is more accurate when it does write a file. 72 of its 83 runs with a file are correct, against 74 of 96 for GLM.

Across tasks, English workspaces beat Hindi in 11 tasks, Hindi wins in 2 (UPI settlement, grievance dedup) and 3 are tied.

Typical wrong fields:

  • child reference ranges ignored for lab flags;
  • capacity chains in room and seed allocation that go wrong after one early slip;
  • revision precedence (an equal revision treated as an override);
  • Unicode length counts after NFC;
  • Devanagari digits not equated with ASCII digits;
  • near-duplicate search queries matched despite the no-spelling-correction rule;
  • accession IDs written without their prefix.

Can these results be trusted?

  • No task defects. Every non-perfect output was diffed against the answer key, and each error that repeated across runs was checked against the notice and the input files. All of them follow from a stated rule. One wording ambiguity remains: in task 07 the inflected खाते may or may not count as the required term खाता. Every run that missed it also had other clear errors, so no outcome depends on it.
  • No infrastructure losses. 9 conditions hit provider transport failures: a short outage and dropped streams. Each was retried once under the same protocol with the primary record kept. All 192 conditions have a graded result.
  • Run-to-run noise is large at the cell level. Two tasks are identical to the preliminary run, and on them 7 of 24 conditions changed result between the two runs. Treat single cells as samples; the factor-level imbalances are the findings.
  • Cost: about $4.43 for all 192 conditions (approximate list rates). Total tokens: 18.9M input and 4.7M output.

Limitations

  • Synthetic data. Hindi and Hinglish text was AI-written with self-review only, not independent human review.
  • One run per condition, 16 tasks, two models: exploratory, with no significance claims and no claims about Indian languages in general.
  • Fixed budgets favour concise models. Nemotron's missing files reflect this protocol's output budget, not its ability under unlimited budgets.

Provenance

v2.1 differs from the preliminary v2 campaign only in a generated value glossary at the end of each notice (data, rules, answers and budgets are identical). It was added after an audit of v2 found two tasks whose notices did not document all value spellings. The change was made after seeing v2 outcomes, and the v2 records and sources are preserved.
